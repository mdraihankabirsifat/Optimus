2. HIGH-LEVEL GAME VISION
======================================================================

Build a simple-to-understand but mechanically original 3D competitive cave-racing game.
Two to five racers enter the same procedurally generated block-shaped cave. The racers
do not know the exit location. They explore the cave, survive hazards, open mystery
boxes, interpret rare clues, manage health, and use a limited gravity-changing power to
walk on walls or ceilings. The first racer to reach the hidden destination wins; later
finishers receive second, third, fourth, and fifth place in arrival order. Racers whose
hearts reach zero are eliminated.

The defining visual moment is that multiple racers can occupy different frames of
reference in the same world. One player may run on the normal floor while another player
is visibly walking on a wall or on the ceiling. A gravity change affects only the racer
who activated it; it never rotates the world or changes gravity for other racers.

The cave should resemble a Minecraft-like, block-built, vacuous square tunnel network.
Use a configurable tunnel cross-section whose initial target is approximately 8 x 8
world/block units. The cave is not a natural smooth spline. It is a cubic/grid-aligned
network built block by block, with corridors, turns, junctions, vertical shafts, loops,
dead ends, chambers, upper/lower passages, hazards, mystery boxes, spawn area, and a
hidden finish area.

The first complete visual mode is Stone Age / Stone Cave. Additional environment modes
are, in this order:
1. Stone Age mode - mandatory first polished mode.
2. Jungle mode - vegetation, roots, damp stone, natural ambience.
3. Dark Cave mode - low visibility, stronger atmospheric horror, restrained light.
4. City Drain mode - concrete/sewer/drain visual treatment.

All modes use the same core mechanics and generator interfaces. Implement the Stone Age
mode completely first. Structure themes as data/resources so Jungle, Dark Cave, and City
Drain can be added sequentially without duplicating gameplay logic. If time allows,
implement them in the order above. Never delay a stable Stone Age build merely to add a
partially working visual mode.

3. THEME INTEGRATION: DEGREE OF FREEDOM
======================================================================

The theme must be part of the rules, not merely the story or title.

Spatial interpretation:
- A straight tunnel where the racer can primarily move forward/backward represents one
  translational degree of freedom.
- A junction allowing travel along two independent axes represents two translational
  degrees of freedom.
- A junction or chamber with horizontal and vertical routes represents three
  translational degrees of freedom.
- Camera look and the Gravity Move introduce rotational freedom and a player-specific
  frame of reference.
- Different racers may simultaneously have different local up vectors, making freedom
  personal rather than global.

Communicate this during play:
- Display a compact current-orientation/gravity indicator in the HUD.
- At suitable junctions, optionally show a subtle contextual indicator such as DOF 1,
  DOF 2, or DOF 3, calculated from meaningful traversable axes. Do not clutter the HUD.
- The tutorial must explain in one or two short panels that greater degrees of freedom
  create more routes but require spending limited Gravity Moves.
- Include a concise About/Theme screen explaining this interpretation for judges.
- Make the core mechanic demonstrable within the first 30-60 seconds of a match.

5. CORE MATCH LOOP
======================================================================

1. Player launches game and reaches a polished home screen.
2. Player chooses Bot Race, Online Race, or Mixed Race.
3. Lobby is configured for 2-5 racers.
4. Host chooses or randomizes a seed and starts the match.
5. All racers spawn in a safe shared starting chamber.
6. A short synchronized countdown plays: 3, 2, 1, GO.
7. The overall match timer starts.
8. Racers explore the same hidden-exit cave.
9. Racers walk/run, look 360 degrees, change personal gravity, avoid hazards, manage
   hearts and Gravity Moves, collect mystery boxes, and use rare clues.
10. The first racer to touch/enter the validated finish trigger is first. Continue
    assigning second through fifth place as racers finish.
11. A racer at zero hearts is eliminated and enters spectator mode.
12. End the match when all non-eliminated racers finish, only one unresolved racer
    remains after placements are determined, or a configurable time limit expires.
13. Show results with placements, finish times, eliminated/DNF status, mystery boxes
    opened, damage taken, and Gravity Moves used.
14. Offer Rematch With Same Seed, New Generated Cave, Return to Lobby, and Main Menu.

The exit must not be shown on the initial map or HUD. Do not provide a full minimap that
reveals unexplored geometry or the exit. The game may show a discovered-only breadcrumb
map later if it is visually and technically safe, but the core version should rely on
spatial exploration, environmental cues, and clue rewards.

6. PLAYER CONTROLLER AND CAMERA
======================================================================

Use Godot CharacterBody3D and GDScript. Build gravity-relative movement; never assume
Vector3.UP is the player's permanent up direction.

Required movement:
- Walk and run/sprint through the cave.
- Camera can look around 360 degrees, Minecraft/FPS style.
- Local human uses a first-person or close immersive camera while other racers have
  visible full character representations. Keep camera perspective modular.
- Remote players and bots must visibly orient to their own gravity and appear to walk on
  floors, walls, or ceilings.
- Movement input is projected onto the plane perpendicular to the player's current
  gravity vector.
- Ground detection, floor snapping, acceleration, friction, jumping if included, and
  collision must all respect the current local up vector.
- Avoid physics tunneling, clipping through tunnel blocks, and jitter on corners.
- Keep player speed fair and identical for equivalent states.
- Include configurable mouse sensitivity, invert-Y option if easy, run toggle/hold, and
  remappable actions through Godot InputMap where practical.

Recommended default bindings, centralized and configurable:
- W/A/S/D: normal gravity-relative movement.
- Mouse: 360-degree look.
- Shift: sprint/run.
- E: interact/open mystery box.
- G: default Gravity Move modifier/action key.
- G + W/A/S/D: choose a 90-degree gravity rotation in a player/camera-relative direction.
- G + Space: choose a 180-degree inversion toward the player's current ceiling.
- Escape: pause/menu.

When G is held, suppress ordinary movement caused by the accompanying W/A/S/D command
long enough to interpret the chord as a gravity command. Give clear on-screen feedback
for the selected gravity direction and remaining Move count.


======================================================================
7. GRAVITY MOVE - NON-NEGOTIABLE CORE MECHANIC
======================================================================

Terminology:
- The special ability may be displayed as Gravity Move or Freedom Shift.
- Its consumable resource is called Move or Move Charge.
- Every racer begins with exactly 5 Move charges.

Rules:
- A racer may spend a Move anywhere at any time, not only at marked zones.
- Each gravity change costs exactly one Move charge.
- Gravity is private per racer.
- A gravity change never rotates the cave/world and never alters another racer's gravity.
- Controls are relative to the player's/camera's current local frame, not fixed world axes.
- Both a 90-degree rotation and a 180-degree inversion cost one Move.
- G + W/A/S/D produces a 90-degree gravity turn toward the corresponding local/camera-
  relative forward/left/back/right direction.
- G + Space produces a 180-degree inversion so the former ceiling can become the new
  walking base.
- A player can use a gravity shift to enter an upper chamber, move through it, and later
  spend another Move to return to a previous orientation.
- Other racers must see this player correctly walking on the ceiling or wall.

Implementation requirements:
- Represent gravity as a normalized vector chosen from the six grid-aligned cardinal
  directions whenever possible: +/-X, +/-Y, +/-Z.
- Maintain a stable orthonormal local basis: local_up = -gravity_direction; local_forward
  is camera forward projected onto the plane perpendicular to local_up; local_right is
  derived consistently.
- Resolve near-degenerate camera projections safely.
- On a gravity command, validate only that a charge exists and the racer is not already
  rotating/eliminated/finished. Do not require a nearby target surface.
- Deduct the charge authoritatively exactly once.
- Temporarily lock movement while transitioning.
- Rotate the player body and camera smoothly, approximately 0.3-0.5 seconds by default.
- Use quaternion/basis interpolation or another method that avoids Euler gimbal lock.
- Preserve a predictable forward direction so the camera does not randomly roll.
- Apply the new gravity continuously after the transition.
- Provide audio, a restrained screen effect, and HUD animation for successful shifts.
- Reject inputs cleanly with feedback when no charges remain.
- Replicate gravity direction, orientation, transition time/state, and charge count.

Unsafe/vacuous behavior:
- Changing gravity toward empty space is allowed.
- The player should move/fall in the newly selected gravity direction until contacting a
  valid surface or reaching a hazard/out-of-bounds condition.
- Special correction supplied by the game designers: if a player uses a 180-degree Move
  while the upper area is vacuous, this must NOT immediately kill/eliminate the player.
  It damages hearts instead. After applying the configured damage, recover the player to
  the last safe grounded transform so play can continue if hearts remain.
- Retain a short history of safe grounded transforms for reliable recovery.
- A general unrecoverable world-boundary violation may eliminate the racer, but the
  protected 180-degree-vacuous case above takes precedence and must cause heart damage,
  not instant elimination.
- Make all boundary/fall damage values configurable and avoid rapid repeated damage.


======================================================================
8. HEALTH, DAMAGE, ELIMINATION, AND SPECTATING
======================================================================

Terminology and rules:
- Every player starts with 5 full hearts.
- Hearts are the visible health representation.
- Refill/revive means restoring lost hearts. It does NOT resurrect an eliminated racer.
- When all hearts are gone, the racer is eliminated for the remainder of that match.
- The design must avoid eliminating players too quickly.

Recommended damage balance, implemented as configurable data:
- Fire contact/tick: 0.5 heart.
- Spider successful attack: 1 heart.
- Unsafe gravity fall/impact or protected vacuous 180-degree failure: 1 heart.
- Strong trap: at most 1.5 hearts.
- After taking damage, grant approximately 1.5 seconds of invulnerability.
- Do not apply repeated damage every physics frame while touching one hazard.
- Use per-source cooldowns and clear audiovisual hit feedback.
- Starting chamber and immediate spawn route must be hazard-free.
- Prevent unfair damage during countdown, gravity transition, loading, or initial spawn.

Optional but approved safety mechanic:
- A rare Second Chance status can prevent fatal damage once and leave the racer with
  0.5 heart. It does not revive a racer after elimination.

Elimination:
- At zero hearts, stop movement/interactions, record elimination time/order, and switch
  the local player to spectator mode.
- Spectator can cycle through active racers without affecting them.
- Clearly mark eliminated racers in HUD/results.
- Never allow health-refill boxes to resurrect an eliminated racer.

Generator hazard constraints:

- Avoid overlapping hazards, boxes, finish trigger, or spawn points.
- Validate each placement against walkable geometry and gravity orientations.

10. MYSTERY BOX SYSTEM
======================================================================

Mystery boxes are placed throughout the cave. A player may interact with a box once.
Opening results are authoritative and synchronized. Prevent two racers from receiving
the same single-use box unless the configured design explicitly supports per-player loot.

Possible rewards and penalties:
- Heart Refill: restore 0.5, 1, or 2 hearts without exceeding 5.
- Move Refill: add one or more Move charges, with a configurable cap.
- Temporary speed boost.
- Temporary shield or damage protection.
- Second Chance status.
- Penalty such as temporary slow, short disorientation, harmless scare, or loss of a Move.
- Rare destination clue.

Clue behavior:
- Destination clues must be rare relative to ordinary outcomes.
- A clue may indicate a coarse direction, relative vertical level, approximate graph
  distance, environmental symbol, or temporary directional pulse.
- It must not permanently reveal the full map.
- Human players and bots receive equivalent clue information.
- Bots may use only clues they personally obtain or legitimately observe.
- Make loot weights data-driven. Use balanced defaults with clue probability clearly low
  (for example around 5%, then tune in playtesting).

Use Heart Refill in player-facing text rather than Revive to avoid implying resurrection.

11. PROCEDURAL 3D CAVE GENERATION
======================================================================

The first map must be fully procedurally generated, not a single handcrafted maze.
However, use modular grid-aligned tunnel/chamber pieces for stability and speed. Fully
procedural means the topology, connections, spawn-to-exit structure, loops, hazards,
boxes, and selected room pieces are generated from a seed; it does not require expensive
runtime marching cubes or smooth terrain meshing.

Generator model:
- Maintain a logical 3D integer lattice/grid.
- A logical cell can connect in six directions: +/-X, +/-Y, +/-Z.
- Generate a connected graph containing corridors, turns, T-junctions, cross-junctions,
  vertical shafts, upper/lower transitions, chambers, dead ends, branches, and self-loops.
- Self-loop means the graph contains cycles, allowing racers to return to earlier regions
  by a different path.
- Use an algorithm such as randomized DFS/Prim/Kruskal for a connected base maze, then
  intentionally braid the maze by adding validated edges/loops and vertical connections.
- Instantiate reusable modular meshes/scenes based on each cell's connection mask.
- Use MultiMesh/static batching/combined collision where appropriate, but favor
  correctness over premature optimization.

Determinism and multiplayer:
- One seed defines the entire match.
- The authoritative host/server chooses and records the seed.
- All clients receive the seed and generator configuration.
- Equivalent versions must generate identical logical maps. Include a generation version
  number/hash in the handshake.
- The server remains authoritative for spawn, finish, hazards, boxes, and important
  interactions even if clients generate matching visuals locally.
- Provide a visible seed on results/rematch screens for debugging and replay.

Required topology invariants:
- Exactly one safe starting chamber and one hidden finish region.
- Spawn and finish must be sufficiently far apart by graph distance.
- At least one guaranteed valid route from spawn to finish.
- At least one meaningful loop/cycle.
- Multiple branches and dead ends.
- Multiple vertical sections requiring or rewarding gravity changes.
- The cave must contain sections demonstrating 1, 2, and 3 translational degrees of
  freedom.
- Ensure no disconnected walkable island unless deliberately decorative and unreachable.
- Avoid overlapping geometry and impossible cell connections.
- Add outer bounds/kill volumes and robust navigation/collision.

Gravity-aware solvability is mandatory:
- Do not validate only ordinary cell connectivity.
- Validate a state graph whose state contains at minimum:
    (logical cave cell, gravity orientation, Move charges remaining)
- Include supported transitions for normal traversal, 90-degree shift, 180-degree shift,
  Move refill if required, and safe/unsafe passage rules.
- Ensure the exit is reachable under the intended starting resource rules, preferably
  within the initial 5 Move charges without relying on random loot.
- Mystery-box Move refills may provide shortcuts but must not be the only unguaranteed way
  to make the base map solvable.
- If validation fails, repair/regenerate deterministically with a capped retry count.
- Add debug output explaining failed generation invariants.
- Provide developer visualization for cells, edges, main valid route, vertical routes,
  spawn/finish, boxes, hazards, and bot knowledge when a debug flag is enabled.

Gameplay readability:
- Do not generate endless visually identical corridors.
- Place landmarks, lighting variations, color accents, cracks, props, or chamber shapes
  without revealing the exit directly.
- The hidden exit may have subtle increasingly noticeable environmental signals such as
  airflow, sound, light, particles, or thematic markings.
- Clues should accelerate informed search; ordinary ambience should prevent completely
  directionless frustration.
- Target a short, replayable match rather than a huge cave. Keep dimensions, branch
  factor, loop count, verticality, and target match duration configurable.

12. OPTIMAL BUT NON-OMNISCIENT BOT AI
======================================================================

Bots play competitively and intelligently, but they must explore without knowing the
exit. They are optimal with respect to legitimately discovered information, not
omniscient.

Strict information boundary:
- The procedural generator/server knows the complete cave.
- A bot AI must not receive the exit coordinate, unexplored global topology, unseen
  hazards, or unopened box contents.
- Expose to each bot only its current observations, discovered cells/edges, remembered
  hazards, opened/seen boxes, personal clues, health, Move charges, and visible racers.
- Architect bot knowledge as a separate discovered-graph model so accidental cheating is
  difficult.

Bot abilities and restrictions:
- Same movement speed and acceleration as humans.
- Same 5 initial Move charges.
- Same health, damage, gravity, collision, hazards, boxes, and elimination rules.
- Can walk/run, choose paths, use 90/180 Gravity Moves, open mystery boxes, obtain clues,
  take damage, finish, and be eliminated.
- Bot gravity/orientation must be fully visible and synchronized.
- Bot does not teleport, phase through geometry, ignore hazards, or query the hidden exit.

Exploration behavior:
- Observe current chamber and connected passages.
- Add visible cells/edges to its personal discovered graph.
- Remember visited cells, traversal costs, hazards, boxes, dead ends, gravity requirements,
  and unsuccessful choices.
- Prefer useful unexplored frontier nodes while penalizing repeated paths.
- Backtrack intelligently when a branch is exhausted.
- Evaluate whether spending a Move produces enough exploration/progress value.
- Use obtained clues to update a probability/priority model without revealing exact exit.
- Once the exit is actually observed/discovered, calculate the shortest safe valid known
  route to it.

Use a deterministic, testable utility model similar to:

  utility = exploration_value
          + clue_alignment_value
          + expected_box_value
          + progress/novelty_value
          - travel_cost
          - known_hazard_risk
          - gravity_move_cost
          - revisit_penalty

Use A*, Dijkstra, frontier-based best-first exploration, or an equivalent graph search on
states such as:

  (cell, gravity_orientation, Moves_remaining, relevant_status)

Do not build machine-learning AI. Use reliable graph/state planning. Add a small natural
reaction delay and smooth physical steering, but do not intentionally make the bot
irrational unless a future difficulty setting requests it. Server controls bots in
online/mixed matches. Offline mode runs the same bot logic locally.

- Server owns lobby membership, match seed/config, countdown, timer, bot decisions,
  Move charge spending, health/damage, mystery-box state/outcomes, clue grants, finish

- Clients may use local input/prediction/interpolation for responsive movement, while the
  server validates plausible movement and periodically corrects drift.
- Do not trust clients to award themselves health, Moves, loot, or placement.
- Use reliable RPC/messages for state-changing events and suitable frequent updates for
  transforms.
- Include sender validation and reject RPCs from the wrong authority.

Synchronization requirements:
- Player ID, display name, avatar/color, human/bot flag.
- Position, velocity, body orientation, local gravity vector, animation state.
- Health/hearts, Move count, buffs/debuffs, eliminated/finished status.
- Match seed, generation version/config checksum, countdown and timer.
- Box opened state/result, hazards where stateful, clue ownership/effect.
- Finish order and timestamps.

Networking quality:
- Smooth remote transforms using interpolation; do not directly snap every frame.
- Handle late join only in lobby unless safe in-match spectating is implemented.
- Time out dead connections and return clear UI errors.
- Host/server should prevent starting with fewer than 2 total racers.
- Room codes must map safely to active lobbies; document any Render/free-tier limitations.
- Show Connecting, Connected, Ready, Reconnecting, and Failed states.
- Avoid blocking the main thread during network operations.
- Add a local debug server configuration for fast testing.

Deployment files:
- Dockerfile for headless Godot server.
- Render configuration/instructions, optionally render.yaml if accurate and maintainable.
- Environment example file with non-secret variables only.
- Health/readiness behavior if practical.
- Exact local server and Render deployment steps in README.md.

======================================================================
19. TESTING AND VALIDATION
======================================================================

Automate what is practical and provide manual test scenes/checklists for the rest.

Cave generation tests:
- Generate at least hundreds of deterministic seeds in headless/debug testing.
- Assert connected spawn/finish.
- Assert spawn-finish minimum distance.
- Assert at least one loop and required verticality.
- Assert no invalid overlapping logical cells/connections.
- Assert gravity-state reachability with 5 initial Moves without random loot dependence.
- Assert the same seed/config produces the same graph hash.
- Assert hazard/box placement constraints.

Gravity tests:
- Execute every 90-degree direction from every cardinal gravity orientation.
- Execute 180-degree inversion from every orientation.
- Chain many rotations without accumulated drift/gimbal lock.
- Verify charge deduction exactly once.
- Verify no shift with zero charges.
- Verify movement and camera remain correct after rotation.
- Verify remote visual orientation.
- Verify protected vacuous 180-degree action damages rather than instantly eliminating.

Health tests:
- Half-heart and full-heart damage.
- Damage cooldown prevents rapid drain.
- Refill clamps to 5 hearts.
- Zero hearts eliminates.
- Heart Refill does not resurrect eliminated racers.
- Second Chance triggers at most once when possessed.

Bot tests:
- Bot cannot access exit before discovery.
- Bot knowledge includes only observed cells.
- Bot backtracks from dead ends.
- Bot uses gravity when valuable and cannot use it at zero Moves.
- Bot reacts to its own clue.
- Bot can finish and be eliminated under normal rules.

Match/network tests:
- Every total racer count from 2 through 5.
- Bot, online, and mixed configurations including 2 humans + 2 bots.
- Same seed/graph hash on server and all clients.
- Box contention resolved once.
- Damage and placement awarded only once.
- Different players maintain independent gravity.
- Disconnect does not crash match.
- Offline Bot Race works with no network.
- Results order ties deterministically using authoritative timestamps.

Build QA:
- Test Windows build on another Windows machine without Godot installed.
- Test web build in a modern browser only if submitted.
- Verify no secrets or private URLs in repository/build.
- Verify settings and menus at common resolutions.
- Verify fast restart for showcase visitors.
- Record known bugs honestly.

20. PERFORMANCE AND SAFETY CONSTRAINTS
======================================================================

- Target stable performance on ordinary student Windows laptops.
- Keep dynamic lights/shadows, particles, physics bodies, and navigation agents modest.
- Use pooled/reused effects where valuable.
- Cull/distance-disable expensive decorations and AI updates.
- Use logical graph navigation for bots instead of expensive full-scene reasoning.
- Keep generation bounded with maximum attempts and clear failure fallback.
- Do not freeze the UI while generating; show seed/generation progress if generation is
  noticeable.
- Avoid unbounded RPCs, unvalidated client calls, or per-frame reliable messages.
- Never commit secrets, API keys, private tokens, or credentials.
- Sanitize player names and room input.
- Gracefully handle missing audio/assets/server rather than crashing.


======================================================================
21. GAME FEEL AND FAIRNESS REQUIREMENTS
======================================================================

- Gravity shifting must feel responsive, intentional, and visually understandable.
- Give a short preview/arrow of selected gravity before or during activation.
- The camera transition must not be nauseating; keep it short and stable.
- Players should understand damage sources immediately.
- Procedural generation must create decisions, not only long identical corridors.
- Rare clues help but do not make all other play irrelevant.
- Random mystery-box outcomes must not produce unavoidable instant elimination.
- Bots play optimally from discovered information but do not cheat.
- Starting positions and initial route access must be fair.
- A racer should not normally be eliminated in the opening moments.
- Five Move charges must create meaningful scarcity while still guaranteeing a base
  solution.
- Use playtest telemetry/debug statistics to tune average match duration, deaths, Move
  usage, box usage, and finish rate.

22. DEFINITION OF DONE / ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
======================================================================

The project is considered feature-complete only when all of the following are true:

Core:
- A human can launch the game, navigate menus, and start a match without the editor.
- A deterministic block-based 3D cave is generated with loops and vertical routes.
- The hidden exit is reachable under gravity-aware validation.
- Player can walk, run, and look 360 degrees.
- G + WASD performs correct 90-degree gravity shifts.
- G + Space performs correct 180-degree inversion.
- Each shift costs one of five initial Moves.
- Different racers visibly use independent gravity.
- Protected vacuous 180-degree failure damages hearts instead of instant elimination.
- Fire, spider, health, elimination, mystery boxes, Heart Refill, and rare clues function.
- Timer and ordered placements function.

Modes:
- One human can race against 1-4 bots offline.
- Bots explore optimally without hidden exit knowledge.
- 2-5 online humans can create/join a room and race.
- Mixed human/bot configurations work, including 2 humans + 2 bots.
- Offline Bot Race remains available if Render/networking fails.

Presentation:
- Home, mode selection, lobby, settings, menu bar/navigation, about/theme, controls,
  credits, pause, HUD, spectator, and results screens function.
- Music and SFX settings work and persist.
- Stone Age mode is coherent and readable.
- Multiple characters/colors are visible.
- README.md, AGENTS.md, future_implementation_suggestion.txt, CREDITS.md, and
  AI_DISCLOSURE.md are complete.
- Render deployment and local server instructions are documented.
- A Windows build can be exported and run independently.
