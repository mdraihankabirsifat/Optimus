SIX WAYS DOWN - FUTURE IMPLEMENTATION SUGGESTIONS
Team Optimus, BUET Robotics Society GameJam 2026

What the jam build does not do yet, roughly in the order worth doing it. Rules that must not
change while doing any of this: 5 hearts, 5 Moves, one Move per shift, private per-racer gravity,
six cardinal gravities, the protected vacuum-180 rule, a seeded solvable cave, and bots that only
know what they have discovered. Offline Bot Race must keep working with no network.


1. ENVIRONMENT MODES

Built in the jam as data (scripts/cave/cave_theme.gd): each environment sets materials,
lighting, fog, ambience pitch and one signature prop. What remains is giving each its own
decor set, hazards and sound, in this order:

1.1 Jungle mode
    - Lift every Stone Age colour, material, light and decor choice out of cave_builder.gd into a
      CaveTheme resource (albedo/roughness per surface, noise settings, fog colour and density,
      ambient light, torch colour, decor kinds and weights, ambience track).
    - Jungle theme: damp green-brown stone, hanging roots on ceilings, ferns in dead ends, water
      drips, softer warm-green light shafts, bird and insect ambience.
    - Root curtains as a soft hazard: slow, never damage.
    - Theme picked in the lobby and sent in the online match config; the generator is unchanged.

1.2 Dark Cave mode
    - Much lower ambient light; racers carry a small personal light that follows their own
      local up. Torches rarer and more valuable as landmarks.
    - Glow-worm ceilings as navigation cues; heavier fog; creaks and distant rumbles.
    - Keep readability: the HUD and the gravity preview must never get harder to read.

1.3 City Drain mode
    - Concrete and brick materials, pipes along walls, grates over shafts (crumble tiles become
      rusting grates), sodium-orange lighting, trickling water.
    - Rats instead of spiders using the same rail logic; steam vents instead of fire.


2. BOTS

- 90-degree wall-walks for bots. CaveValidator already models (cell, any of 6 gravities) and
  tests/test_wallwalk.gd proves the controller can walk up a wall into a shaft. The planner would
  search six gravities, return (cell, gravity) steps, and the controller would request the
  gravity the next step needs. Measure with test_bot and race_diag before shipping.
- Difficulty personalities that change choices, never information: a cautious bot that hoards
  Moves, a gambler that shifts early, a box hunter that detours for boxes it has seen.
- A proper utility score (exploration, clue alignment, expected box value, novelty, travel cost,
  hazard risk, Move cost, revisit penalty) with weights tuned from logged races.
- Bots reading other racers: following someone who just shifted into a shaft is information any
  human could also see.


3. CAVE AND LEVELS

- More modules and landmarks: collapsed halls, bridges across tall chambers, spiral shafts,
  large multi-cell chambers with pillars to walk around on any surface.
- Deliberate 90-degree puzzles: wall-only routes where a floor is missing, so wall-walking is
  sometimes the best route rather than just a trick.
- Stronger Move economy: non-aligned shafts so one inversion no longer climbs everything.
- Stronger exit cues that grow with proximity: airflow particles, a faint hum, warm light.
- Developer visualisation overlay: cells, edges, spine, vertical links, hazards, boxes, and a
  selected bot's discovered graph drawn in 3D behind a debug flag.


4. HAZARDS, BOXES, ABILITIES

- More hazards: falling stalactites, rolling boulders along corridors, flooding shafts.
- More box outcomes: short disorientation, harmless scare, a one-shot "gravity lock" shield,
  a directional pulse clue, an approximate graph-distance clue.
- Strong traps of up to 1.5 hearts, always telegraphed.
- Loot tables per theme, all data-driven in AppConfig or a resource.


5. CHARACTERS, COSMETICS, ACCESSIBILITY

- Several character silhouettes, hats and trails; choose a colour in the lobby.
- Colour-blind palettes, a high-contrast HUD option, UI scale slider, subtitles for audio cues.
- Reduced-motion option that shortens the camera slerp and removes screen effects entirely.
- Controller support: sticks for move and look, a held shoulder button as the gravity modifier,
  face buttons for directions, remappable through InputMap.


6. MAP AND SPECTATING

- The discovered-only map exists (M). Next: per-level slicing controls, marks for boxes you saw,
  and a shared team map in a co-op mode. Never the exit.
- Spectator improvements: free camera, picture-in-picture of the leader, a race timeline.
- Replays: record inputs or poses per racer from the server and play back a whole race.


7. MODES

- Time Trial leaderboards per seed (local now; online later).
- Daily cave online leaderboard.
- Co-op: a team shares Moves and must get everyone out.
- Tag / hunter mode: one racer hunts the others, every catch transfers the role.
- Ranked play and matchmaking only if there is ever a real player base; persistent accounts only
  then, never for the jam build.


8. NETWORKING

- Client-side reconciliation: replay local inputs after a server correction instead of snapping.
- Lag compensation for box and finish-line contention using server timestamps of client poses.
- Rejoin a race in progress after a drop (keep the racer slot for a short grace period).
- Spectate a running race from the lobby.
- Server health endpoint for Render readiness checks (a small HTTP responder beside the WebSocket).
- Scale out: one room per process behind a tiny room directory, or several Render instances with
  room codes that encode the instance.
- Bandwidth: pack snapshots into PackedByteArray, delta-compress, send far racers less often.
- Region selection and a public server list.


9. PERFORMANCE AND PLATFORMS

- Profile on a low-end Intel iGPU laptop: draw calls, light counts per object, particle counts.
- Distance-disable decor, spider animation and torch flicker outside a few cells.
- Occlusion by cave cell: only draw cells within N hops of the camera.
- Web: smaller textures, fewer lights, audio worklet checks, a loading progress bar.
- Mobile only with a control scheme designed for it.
